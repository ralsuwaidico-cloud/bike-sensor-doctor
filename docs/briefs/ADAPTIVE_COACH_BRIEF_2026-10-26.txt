Claude, I want to upgrade Cyclift’s coaching architecture into a genuinely adaptive hybrid-athlete training system.

The app must automatically adjust future cycling and strength sessions based on completed workouts, post-workout feedback, historical performance, available equipment and my training preferences.

This must be integrated with the existing Cyclift work, not implemented as a disconnected feature.

A. Personalized hybrid-athlete onboarding

When the user selects Hybrid Athlete, ask:

1. Primary goal: strength, cycling endurance, balanced hybrid, general fitness or custom.
2. Total training days per week.
3. Number of cycling sessions per week.
4. Number of strength sessions per week.
5. Preferred rest days and fixed/flexible training days.
6. Preferred strength split: Full Body, Push/Pull/Legs, Upper/Lower or Custom.
7. Frequency of each workout type.
8. Session duration and weekly time budget for each sport.
9. Available gym/home equipment.
10. Existing workout templates the user wants to preserve.

Do not assume Push/Pull/Legs always requires six days or Full Body always requires three.

Allow multiple sessions on the same day when the selected frequency exceeds available training days.

For custom splits, support examples such as:

* Two full-body sessions plus one core/joint-strengthening session.
* One upper-body, one lower-body and one mobility/strength session.
* Three custom lifting templates.
* Any combination of cycling and strength consistent with the user’s limits.

Users must be able to choose their own fixed days and rest days.

B. Equipment-aware strength programming

Maintain an equipment profile.

Allow users to select exact available equipment and save their existing exercises and templates.

The coach must build around those exercises.

Never prescribe equipment the user has marked unavailable.

Recommend compatible substitutes when necessary, but do not silently replace exercises.

C. Post-workout feedback

After every cycling or strength session, ask:

* Performance: 1–10, where 10 means excellent performance relative to expectations.
* Perceived effort: 1–10, where 10 means maximal effort.
* Pain or unusual discomfort: none, normal soreness or concerning pain.

Optionally allow short notes.

Keep this check-in fast and simple.

For strength sessions, also analyze:

* Exercises performed.
* Sets, reps and weights.
* Completion rate.
* RPE or reps in reserve where available.
* Previous performance on the same exercises.

For cycling, analyze available:

* Duration and intensity.
* Heart rate and heart-rate zones.
* Power and cadence.
* Training load.
* Perceived effort.
* Recovery trends.
* Data reliability and source quality.

Never invent missing metrics.

D. Automatic adaptation engine

Build a deterministic, testable adaptation engine that combines objective training data with subjective post-workout feedback.

The engine must:

1. Compare current performance against the athlete’s personal baseline.
2. Consider both cycling and lifting workload.
3. Detect possible accumulated fatigue or improving performance.
4. Adjust future training volume or intensity conservatively.
5. Maintain the user’s chosen training days and overall time limits.
6. Respect fixed sessions and selected strength splits.
7. Avoid automatic progression when pain or unreliable data is reported.
8. Avoid overreacting to one unusual workout.
9. Use rolling trends and historical performance where available.
10. Explain each automatic adjustment.

Use appropriate sports-science principles, but do not claim that subjective scores alone diagnose recovery, overtraining or injury.

The coach should be able to change:

* Strength repetitions.
* Strength weights, when justified by actual logged performance.
* Number of sets.
* Cycling intensity targets.
* Interval repetitions.
* Session duration.
* Recovery emphasis.

For low-confidence situations, maintain the existing plan and request additional feedback.

E. Automatic versus approval-required changes

Automatically apply small, bounded changes to future sessions.

Require approval for:

* Changing fixed training days.
* Adding or removing weekly sessions.
* Changing the user’s strength split.
* Replacing saved exercises.
* Exceeding time budgets.
* Major training-load increases.

All automatic changes must be visible and reversible.

Include “Why this changed?” and “Restore original plan.”

F. Continuous learning loop

The system should follow:

Plan → Perform → Log → Rate → Analyze → Adapt → Repeat.

The coach must learn the athlete’s individual response over time.

Separate:

* Rules and constraints.
* Quantitative training analysis.
* Optional AI-generated explanations and broader recommendations.

Do not pretend a language model is providing reasoning unless an actual model is integrated.

Use the deterministic engine as the reliable fallback when offline or when AI services are unavailable.

G. Testing and delivery

Create tests for:

* Five training days with two cycling and three lifting sessions.
* Custom split with two full-body days and one core/joints day.
* Six-day Push/Pull/Legs with cycling overlap.
* Limited equipment.
* Repeated high-effort sessions.
* Improving strength performance.
* Cycling fatigue affecting strength recommendations.
* Strength fatigue affecting cycling recommendations.
* Missed workouts.
* Missing sensors.
* Pain reports.
* Preserving fixed days and time budgets.
* Reverting automatic changes.

Preserve existing profiles, workout templates, ride history, scheduled workouts and completed logs.

Integrate this into the current Cyclift project without disrupting the release preparation.

The goal is for Cyclift to behave like a personal hybrid-athlete coach that automatically improves my training program based on my actual responses—not a static workout generator.
