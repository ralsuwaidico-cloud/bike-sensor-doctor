 BikeComputer | Hybrid Athlete
 Claude implementation brief - iOS UX, onboarding, planning, logging and adaptive coaching
 Instruction: Implement the changes in the actual iOS application and deliver a TestFlight build. Use the
 supplied screenshots and the existing Hybrid Athlete proposal as references. Preserve existing ride
 recording, BLE, device sync, routes, gauges, and history. Do not substitute mockups for working
 functionality.


 1. Fix visual defects throughout the app
  • Strength Log selection buttons currently wrap "Upper body", "Lower body" and "Custom" awkwardly.
    Replace with a responsive 2- or 3-column selection layout using concise labels: Full Body, Upper, Lower,
    Push, Pull, Custom.
  • Audit onboarding, preferred-day chips, weekly calendar, workout cards, summaries, Profile and modal
    sheets for clipped text, inconsistent spacing, off-screen content and overlapping elements.
  • Support small iPhone screens, safe areas, scrolling, Dynamic Type and both themes. Do not solve by
    shrinking all text to unreadable sizes. Maintain touch targets of approximately 44 points.


 2. Onboarding: specify exactly what happens each day
  • Keep guest entry simple: ask for name only, then offer optional progressive training setup. Allow Cycling,
    Strength or Hybrid Athlete, editable later.
  • For each Monday-Sunday day, assign Cycling, Strength, Both, Rest or Flexible. Allow different activities on
    the same day, and mark each assignment Fixed or Flexible.
  • Ask the type and planned duration for each activity. Cycling choices: Zone 2/endurance, tempo,
    threshold, intervals, recovery, long ride, custom. Strength choices: full body, upper, lower, push, pull,
    legs, custom routine.
  • Show a readable weekly preview before saving. Confirm that chosen frequency, per-day durations and
    separate cycling/lifting weekly budgets are mutually consistent.
  • When constraints conflict, explain the conflict and ask which to relax; never silently add extra days or
    exceed available hours.


 3. Searchable strength routines
  • Provide an exercise search by name, equipment, movement and muscle group. Support sets, repetitions,
    weight, bodyweight, unilateral movements, supersets, optional RPE and notes.
  • Let users create, rename, edit and reuse workout templates, including prefilled values from the last
    session. Quick Log remains available for session type, duration and effort without exercise detail.
  • Do not infer precise muscle coverage from generic labels such as "Full Body"; mark missing evidence as
    unknown.


 4. Adaptive coaching across cycling AND lifting
  • Use actual completed sessions, not merely scheduled sessions. Analyze cycling frequency, duration,
    intensity distribution, HR/power/cadence where available, strength frequency, exercise progression and
    push/pull, squat/hinge, single-leg and core coverage.
  • Identify potential interference: hard leg workouts adjacent to demanding rides; suggest changes within
    the user-approved schedule and recovery constraints, without medical claims.
  • Provide one clear Home recommendation, a compact table (type, duration, intensity, expected benefit),
    and a "Why?" explanation grounded in available data. Label estimates and insufficient evidence.

 5. Weekly Plan: clean, feasible and editable
  • Make the week readable: seven day cards with concise icons and formatted durations such as "1 h 5
    min". Tap any day to view, add, edit, move, replace, skip or complete its workouts.
  • Enforce separate cycling and lifting time budgets at plan generation time. Do not generate 2 h 5 min
    against a 2 h cycling limit, or 2 h 10 min against a 2 h lifting limit.
  • Show a compact summary: rides planned/completed, lifts planned/completed, time budget, and one
    prioritized action. Move lengthy red warning paragraphs behind "Why?" or "Fix Plan".
  • Explain meaningful changes between weeks, especially when training mode or schedule changes.
    Preserve historical plans and manually edited sessions.


 6. History, sync and personalization
  • Below the weekly plan, add a timeline of previous rides and strength workouts with status: planned,
    completed, skipped, missed or imported. Tap for available metrics and exercise progression.
  • Import permitted Apple Health activities and ESP32 rides with stable source IDs and deduplication.
    Preserve offline strength logs and sync later without duplication.
  • If workouts are repeatedly missed, offer to revise availability rather than repeatedly imposing the same
    plan. Include a weekly review comparing planned vs completed work and a suggested next-week
    adjustment.
  • Maintain phone-authoritative shared athlete settings after pairing, with safe conflict handling; preserve
    independent ESP32 ride records and offline operation.


 7. Acceptance tests and delivery
  • Test layout on compact and large iPhones, large text, dark/light themes; verify no clipping, overlapping
    controls or accidental taps.
  • Test fixed/flexible day behavior, combined sessions, exact budget limits, quick/detailed strength logging,
    templates, edits, undo, offline sync and duplicate prevention.
  • Verify coach recommendations cite actual data and never count uncompleted sessions as completed.
    Confirm that cycling time is not counted as lifting volume.
  • Regression-test existing phone-only rides, ESP32 recording, Bluetooth, Apple Watch/HR, maps, Ride Now
    gauges, ride history and profile sync.
  • Deliver the TestFlight build number, changelog, automated test results, what was physically tested,
    outstanding limitations and a concise manual QA checklist. Do not claim simulator-tested behavior is
    hardware-verified.


 Suggested priority order
 P0: layout defects and schedule/budget correctness. P1: per-day sport assignment and exercise
 search/templates. P2: integrated coaching, timeline and weekly review. Keep the first release functional
 and incremental.
